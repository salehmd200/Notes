## Key Knowledge

### Mood Disorders

- **MDD:** depressive episode ≥2 weeks with clinically significant symptoms/impairment.
- **Bipolar I:** requires ≥1 manic episode; a depressive episode is not required.
- **Bipolar II:** hypomania + major depressive episode; no history of mania.
- **Cyclothymia:** ≥2 years of fluctuating hypomanic/depressive symptoms that never meet full episode criteria.
- Antidepressant monotherapy can precipitate **mania** in bipolar disorder.
- Important diagnostic distinction: brief/limited mood elevation without sufficient duration, impairment, or qualifying depressive history does not automatically establish bipolar II.

### Psychotic Disorders

- **Brief psychotic disorder:** <1 month.
- **Schizophreniform:** 1–6 months.
- **Schizophrenia:** ≥6 months of illness, including ≥1 month of active-phase symptoms.
- **Schizoaffective disorder:** mood episode + schizophrenia-spectrum illness, with **≥2 weeks of psychosis without mood symptoms**.
- **Delusional disorder:** persistent delusion with relatively preserved functioning and without the broader schizophrenia syndrome.
- **Paranoid personality disorder:** pervasive mistrust/suspiciousness rather than a discrete fixed delusional belief.
- Visual hallucinations or psychosis-like symptoms should prompt consideration of **delirium, substances, neurological disease, and other medical causes**.

### Anxiety Disorders

- **GAD:** excessive, difficult-to-control worry across multiple domains for ≥6 months.
- **Panic disorder:** recurrent unexpected panic attacks + persistent concern or behavioral change.
- **Social anxiety disorder:** fear of scrutiny/embarrassment in social or performance situations.
- **Specific phobia:** fear centered on a particular object or situation.
- **Agoraphobia:** fear/avoidance of situations where escape or help may be difficult, such as public transport, crowds, or certain open/enclosed spaces.
- Important differential: panic-associated avoidance is not automatically a specific phobia.

### OCD & Related Disorders

- **OCD:** intrusive obsessions and/or compulsions that are typically ego-dystonic and distressing.
- **OCPD:** pervasive perfectionism/control/rigidity that is generally ego-syntonic.
- Violent intrusive thoughts can occur in OCD and **do not by themselves indicate intent to harm**.
- Postpartum intrusive harm thoughts with distress, avoidance, and intact reality testing can represent **postpartum OCD**.
- OCD treatment commonly involves **ERP/CBT and SSRIs**, often at higher doses than those used for depression.

### Trauma & Stressor Disorders

- **Acute stress disorder:** trauma-related symptoms lasting 3 days–1 month.
- **PTSD:** symptoms persist >1 month.
- **Adjustment disorder:** maladaptive response to an identifiable stressor without meeting criteria for another disorder.

### Personality Disorders

**Cluster A**

- Paranoid
- Schizoid
- Schizotypal

**Cluster B**

- Antisocial
- Borderline
- Histrionic
- Narcissistic

**Cluster C**

- Avoidant
- Dependent
- OCPD

High-yield distinctions:

- **Schizoid:** little interest in relationships; comfortable alone.
- **Avoidant:** wants relationships but fears rejection/criticism.
- **Schizotypal:** eccentricity, unusual beliefs/magical thinking, perceptual abnormalities.
- **Borderline:** instability, abandonment sensitivity, splitting, self-harm/suicidality.
- **OCPD:** perfectionism, rigidity, excessive control; generally ego-syntonic.
- **DBT** is a key evidence-based treatment for borderline personality disorder.

### Somatic & Related Disorders

- **Somatic symptom disorder:** significant/excessive thoughts, anxiety, or behaviors related to actual somatic symptoms.
- **Illness anxiety disorder:** preoccupation with serious illness despite minimal or absent somatic symptoms.
- **Functional neurological/conversion disorder:** neurological symptoms incompatible with recognized neurological disease; not intentionally produced.
- **Factitious disorder:** intentional falsification/production of illness to assume the sick role.
- **Malingering:** intentional production of symptoms for external gain.

### Sleep-Wake Disorders

- **Narcolepsy:** excessive daytime sleepiness, sleep attacks, often cataplexy, and abnormal REM regulation.
- **NREM parasomnias:** sleepwalking and night terrors.
- **REM sleep behavior disorder:** dream enactment.
- **Insomnia:** difficulty initiating/maintaining sleep or non-restorative sleep with daytime consequences.
- **CBT-I** is an important first-line treatment for chronic insomnia.

### Impulse-Control Disorders

- **Intermittent explosive disorder:** recurrent disproportionate aggressive outbursts.
- **Kleptomania:** recurrent stealing of objects not needed for personal use/value.
- **Pyromania:** recurrent deliberate fire-setting associated with fascination/tension/pleasure and not primarily motivated by external gain.
- CBT is an important treatment approach.

### Substance Use Disorders

Core intoxication/withdrawal patterns:

|Substance|Intoxication|Withdrawal|
|---|---|---|
|Alcohol|Disinhibition, ataxia, slurred speech|Tremor, autonomic symptoms, seizures, delirium tremens|
|Opioids|Euphoria, respiratory depression, **miosis**|Yawning, lacrimation, diarrhea, **mydriasis**|
|Cocaine/stimulants|Agitation, hypertension, mydriasis, paranoia|Fatigue, depression, craving|
|Cannabis|Euphoria, red eyes, increased appetite|Irritability, insomnia, appetite changes|
|Benzodiazepines|Sedation, ataxia|Anxiety, insomnia, seizures|

Alcohol withdrawal timeline:

- ~6–24 h: early withdrawal
- ~12–48 h: withdrawal seizures
- ~48–96 h: delirium tremens

Management:

- Alcohol withdrawal → **benzodiazepines** + appropriate supportive care.
- Opioid overdose → **naloxone**.
- Opioid withdrawal/maintenance → methadone or buprenorphine are important treatments.
- Thiamine is important in patients at risk of deficiency.

### Neurocognitive Disorders

- **Delirium:** acute onset, fluctuating course, impaired attention; medical emergency.
- **Major neurocognitive disorder/dementia:** usually gradual cognitive decline interfering with independence.
- Alzheimer disease is a major cause of dementia.
- **Sundowning:** worsening confusion/agitation/wandering in the evening, especially in dementia.

### Psychiatric Emergencies & High-Yield Syndromes

#### Catatonia

- Features include stupor, mutism, waxy flexibility, negativism, echolalia.
- **Lorazepam** is generally first-line.
- ECT is important for severe/refractory cases.

#### Wernicke encephalopathy

- Classic features: **confusion + ataxia + ocular abnormalities**.
- Treat promptly with **thiamine**.

#### Serotonin syndrome

- Mental-status changes + autonomic instability + neuromuscular hyperactivity.
- **Clonus/hyperreflexia** are particularly useful clues.
- Can occur with serotonergic combinations such as an SSRI + linezolid.

#### Neuroleptic malignant syndrome

- Antipsychotic exposure + **fever + severe rigidity + autonomic instability + elevated CK**.
- Distinguish from uncomplicated antipsychotic-induced parkinsonism.

#### Antipsychotic EPS

- **Acute dystonia:** treat with anticholinergic medication such as benztropine/diphenhydramine.
- **Parkinsonism:** rigidity/bradykinesia/tremor; consider dose reduction/switch and appropriate symptomatic treatment.
- **Akathisia:** subjective inner restlessness/pacing; **propranolol** is commonly used.
- **Tardive dyskinesia:** delayed involuntary movements; consider VMAT2 inhibitor treatment.

### Assessment Preferences

- **MCQs are the default assessment format.**
- Medium difficulty initially.
- Increase difficulty according to performance.
- Avoid overly easy or impossibly difficult questions.
- Do not put diagnostic hints in question titles.
- After each set:
    - Score answers.
    - Explain mistakes.
    - Identify specific knowledge gaps.
    - Target those gaps in subsequent questions.

### Preferred Teaching Style

- Prefer **crash-course teaching** over schedule-style rapid review.
- Explain concepts simply despite the user's medical background.
- Aim for high-level clinical reasoning rather than memorization alone.

## Workflows / Methods

### Adaptive MCQ Workflow

1. Present 5 MCQs.
2. User submits answers without explanations.
3. Grade each question.
4. Explain both correct and incorrect answers where useful.
5. Update the knowledge-gap map.
6. Build subsequent questions around:
    - Previous errors
    - Closely related differentials
    - Management decisions
    - High-yield exam traps
7. Gradually increase integration and difficulty.

### Alternative Drill Formats

Useful alternatives when desired:

- **Rapid diagnosis drills:** short vignette → diagnosis.
- **Differential diagnosis battles:** distinguish overlapping disorders.
- **Clinical cases:** progressively reveal information and require management decisions.
- **Viva-style questions:** concise oral-exam responses.
- **Flash recall:** rapid identification of diagnoses, drugs, adverse effects, or criteria.
- **Compare-and-contrast:** e.g., OCD vs OCPD or schizophrenia vs schizoaffective disorder.
- **Supercondensed reviews:** rapid high-yield revision.
