Ventilator Adjustment — Quick Reference

Core principle: Minute ventilation = RR × Vt. This controls CO₂ elimination. PEEP and FiO₂ control oxygenation.

Respiratory alkalosis (low PaCO₂, high pH):
Patient is over-ventilated → ↓RR (if Vt is already lung-protective at 6 mL/kg IBW, RR is the lever to pull). If Vt is too high (>8 mL/kg), fix Vt instead.

Respiratory acidosis (high PaCO₂, low pH):
Patient is under-ventilated → ↑RR (if Vt is already appropriate).

Hypoxemia (low PaO₂):

	•	First-line: ↑PEEP (improves alveolar recruitment)
	•	↑FiO₂ also helps, but avoid staying at toxic levels (near 100%) long-term
	•	If already maxed on FiO₂ → ↑PEEP is the next move

Lung-protective ventilation target:
Vt = 4–8 mL/kg ideal body weight (6 mL/kg is the standard target). Don’t correct CO₂ problems by pushing Vt outside this range if RR can be adjusted instead.

Decision shortcut:



|Problem            |Check Vt first   |Then adjust                                          |
|-------------------|-----------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
|Alkalosis (CO₂ low)|If Vt appropriate|↓RR                                                  |
|Alkalosis (CO₂ low)|If Vt too high   |↓Vt                                                  |
|Acidosis (CO₂ high)|If Vt appropriate|↑RR                                                  |
|Acidosis (CO₂ high)|If Vt too low    |↑Vt                                                  |
|Hypoxemia          |—                |↑PEEP first, then ↑FiO₂ (or vice versa if FiO₂ maxed)|
