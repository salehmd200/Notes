# Endocrinology: Recurring Physiological Patterns

## 1. Binding Proteins vs. Free Hormone

- Increased binding protein → increased **total** hormone concentration, but **free/active** hormone stays normal (assuming intact feedback and equilibration).
- Examples: TBG (OCPs, pregnancy) and T4; CBG and cortisol; SHBG and testosterone/estrogen; albumin and calcium.
- Clinical implication: total levels can mislead if binding protein status isn’t accounted for — check free hormone when binding protein levels are abnormal.

## 2. Co-Secreted Markers: Thyroglobulin & C-Peptide

- Both are byproducts of the final step of hormone synthesis/processing.
- Thyroglobulin: proteolyzed within the thyroid follicle to release T4/T3.
- C-peptide: cleaved from proinsulin alongside insulin.
- Clinical use: distinguishing **endogenous vs. exogenous** hormone source.
  - Low thyroglobulin + high thyroid hormone → exogenous intake (factitious thyrotoxicosis).
  - Low C-peptide + high insulin/hypoglycemia → exogenous insulin (not insulinoma).

## 3. Deficiency vs. Resistance (Unifying Framework)

- Many endocrine disorders pair a **deficiency** state with a **resistance** state affecting the same hormone/receptor axis.
- In resistance states, the upstream trophic hormone is typically **elevated**, because feedback loops sense hormonal *effect*, not just hormone level.

|Hormone        |Deficiency                                          |Resistance                                   |
|---------------|----------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
|ADH            |Central DI                                          |Nephrogenic DI                               |
|Insulin        |Type 1 DM                                           |Type 2 DM                                    |
|PTH            |Hypoparathyroidism                                  |Pseudohypoparathyroidism                     |
|GH             |GH deficiency                                       |Laron syndrome (GH receptor defect)          |
|Thyroid hormone|Hypothyroidism                                      |Resistance to thyroid hormone (THRB mutation)|
|Androgen       |Androgen deficiency                                 |Androgen insensitivity syndrome              |
|Vitamin D      |Deficiency / VDDR type 1 (1α-hydroxylase deficiency)|VDDR type 2 (VDR mutation)                   |

- **Mechanistic note:** Nephrogenic DI (V2 receptor) and Pseudohypoparathyroidism (PTH receptor) both involve Gs-protein-coupled receptors signaling via adenylate cyclase/cAMP. PHP type 1a specifically involves a Gsα defect (GNAS mutation), which can cause resistance to *multiple* Gs-coupled hormones simultaneously (e.g., also TSH, gonadotropins).

## 4. DI vs. SIADH — Mirror-Image Pathophysiology

- DI: ADH deficiency/resistance → dilute polyuria.
- SIADH: ADH excess → concentrated oliguria, hyponatremia.
- Same axis, opposite direction.
- Related differential: **hypotonic polyuria** workup includes central DI, nephrogenic DI, and primary polydipsia — distinguished via water deprivation test.

## 5. Additional Related Concepts

- **PTHrP** (malignancy-associated hypercalcemia): activates the PTH receptor but is not detected by standard PTH immunoassays → hypercalcemia with *low* PTH, indicating a non-PTH-driven cause.
- **Primary vs. Secondary/Tertiary Failure Pattern** (applies across thyroid, adrenal, gonadal axes):
  - Primary (gland failure): low hormone + high trophic hormone.
  - Secondary/tertiary (pituitary/hypothalamic failure): both low.

## Key Insights

1. **Deficiency vs. resistance** is the master framework — nearly every endocrine axis has a “not enough hormone” disorder and a “hormone can’t act” disorder, with the latter marked by an elevated trophic hormone.
1. **Free vs. total hormone** matters whenever binding proteins are altered (pregnancy, OCPs, liver/renal disease, albumin changes) — don’t trust total levels in isolation.
1. **Co-secreted cleavage products** (thyroglobulin, C-peptide) are the clinical tool for telling endogenous production apart from exogenous administration.
1. **Gs-coupled receptor signaling** links seemingly unrelated resistance syndromes (nephrogenic DI, pseudohypoparathyroidism) at the mechanistic level, not just by analogy.
1. **Primary vs. secondary failure logic** (gland vs. central) is a reusable diagnostic lens applicable across thyroid, adrenal, gonadal, and calcium axes.
